🌈 Why are stars different colors?

A star's color depends mainly on its temperature, with red stars cooler and bluish-white stars hotter.

The big idea

A star's color comes mainly from its surface temperature: cooler stars look red, hotter stars look bluish-white.

How it works

🔴

Cool and red

Red M-type stars are about 2,100 to 3,400°C on the surface.

🟠

Orange

K-type stars are about 3,400 to 4,900°C.

💙

Hot and blue

Bluish-white stars like Vega are hotter than orange or red ones.

More about the solar system

  • Antares is a red star, and Arcturus is an orange star.
  • Many stars look white because they are too faint for the eye to see color.
  • Binoculars or a telescope can show a star's color.

Words to know

Temperature
How hot or cold something is.
Telescope
A tool that makes faraway things look closer.

🧪 Try it yourself

On a clear night, look at the brightest stars. Can you spot one that looks slightly red or orange?
