🌐 How do satellites stay up?

Satellites do not float. They keep falling around Earth fast enough that they never hit the ground.

The big idea

A satellite is always falling around Earth, but it moves sideways so fast that Earth curves away beneath it.

How it works

🚀

Sideways speed

It is released moving at up to 18,000 mph sideways.

🌍

Curved Earth

Earth curves away as it falls, so it never lands.

🌬️

Thin air

Little air at orbit height keeps friction low.

More about the solar system

  • Lower orbits meet thicker air, so satellites there last only weeks to months.
  • The former U.S.S.R. launched the first satellite in late 1957.
  • Satellites travel about 5 miles per second.

Words to know

Satellite
An object that orbits a planet.
Drag
Air friction that slows things down.

🧪 Try it yourself

Throw a ball gently, then harder. The harder you throw, the farther it goes. A satellite is thrown so fast that Earth curves away before it lands!
