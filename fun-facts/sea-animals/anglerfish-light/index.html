💡 How do anglerfish make light?

Anglerfish have a glowing lure to find food in the dark deep sea.

The big idea

Female anglerfish have a glowing lure on a rod above their mouth. The light comes from bioluminescent bacteria living in the lure.

How it works

💡

A living lamp

The lure, called the esca, is full of glowing bacteria that need a cue from the fish to make light.

📏

Big girl, tiny boy

Females reach up to 29 cm (11 in). Males are about 2.8 cm (1 in).

🔗

Males attach

A male bites onto a larger female, and several males can fuse with one female.

More about anglerfishs

  • Males have no lure, but they are more muscular, so they can swim far to find mates.
  • Fused males connect to the female's skin and blood, and lose their own eyes and organs.
  • Anglerfish live in a part of the ocean with little fresh food.

Words to know

Bioluminescent
Able to make its own light.
Esca
The glowing lure at the end of an anglerfish's rod.

🧪 Try it yourself

Turn off the lights and use a torch to find a small toy across the room. An anglerfish does the same thing with its own living light!
