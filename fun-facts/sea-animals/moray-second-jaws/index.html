😮 Which fish has a second set of jaws?

Moray eels have a second set of jaws hidden in their throat.

The big idea

Moray eels have a second set of jaws in their throat, called pharyngeal jaws. They shoot forward to grab prey and pull it down.

How it works

🎣

A launching jaw

When the main jaws close, the second set shoots into the mouth, grabs prey with backward-pointing teeth and drags it into the throat.

🪨

Tight spaces

Morays live in narrow rocky alcoves, so they cannot use suction feeding.

🎥

Seen on video

Scientists at UC Davis used high-speed and X-ray video of reticulated morays.

More about moray eels

  • Morays are the only fish, and the only backboned animals, known to use this ballistic technique.
  • The prey is bitten twice and swallowed in a fraction of a second.
  • Their jaw muscles are much longer than those of other fish.

Words to know

Pharyngeal jaws
A second set of jaws inside the throat.
Alcove
A small hollow space in the rocks.

🧪 Try it yourself

Tuck a small toy under your sleeve and slide it out in a flash. That is like the moray's second set of jaws shooting forward!
