The big idea

An octopus has three hearts: two small gill hearts and one large main (systemic) heart. Its blood is blue, and when an octopus swims, its main heart briefly stops beating.

How each heart works

HeartHow manyJob
❤️ Gill hearts2Push blood through the gills, where it picks up oxygen from seawater.
❤️ Main heart1Pumps the oxygen-rich blood around the rest of the body.

Why blue blood?

Our blood is red because it carries oxygen with iron (in a protein called hemoglobin). Octopus blood uses copper (in hemocyanin), which turns blue when it carries oxygen. Copper works well in cold water with little oxygen, but it makes the blood thicker, so it needs extra pumping power.

Why three hearts?

🫧

Thick blood

Hemocyanin blood is harder to push through tiny gill vessels, so two helper hearts give a boost.

🌊

Active hunters

Octopuses need lots of oxygen for their big brains and strong arms.

🏊

Swimming pauses the main heart

When jetting through water, the main heart stops, so swimming tires the octopus. It crawls along the sea floor instead.

More octopus facts

  • Octopuses can squeeze through any gap larger than their beak.
  • They can change colour in a fraction of a second.
  • They squirt ink to confuse enemies.

Words to know

Gills
Body parts that take oxygen from water.
Hemocyanin
The copper-based protein that makes octopus blood blue.
Hemoglobin
The iron-based protein that makes our blood red.

🧪 Try it yourself

Put two fingers on your wrist or neck and feel your one heart beating. Then jump 20 times and check again. Do you feel it faster? An octopus has three hearts doing that job!
