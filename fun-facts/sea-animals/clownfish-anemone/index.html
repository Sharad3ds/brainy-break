🪸 How do clownfish live in stinging anemones?

Clownfish live safely among stinging tentacles. Scientists found part of the secret.

The big idea

Clownfish avoid anemone stings partly because their skin mucus has very little sialic acid, a sugar that can trigger the anemone's stinging cells. Scientists think other things help too.

How it works

🍬

Low sugar

Anemonefish keep very low levels of sialic acid in their skin mucus. Their organs, like the brain and gut, keep normal levels.

🦋

Timing matters

Young larvae that are not ready for an anemone have normal levels and get stung. After they change, the levels drop.

🔬

Still a mystery

Researchers think this is one part of a complex partnership. The full answer has not been proven yet.

More about clownfishs

  • Sea anemones also lack these sugars in their own mucus, which probably stops them stinging themselves.
  • Young domino damselfish, which also live with anemones when little, show the same lower levels.
  • Scientists suggest enzymes or bacteria in the mucus may help break the sugar down.

Words to know

Anemone
A soft sea animal with stinging tentacles that looks like a flower.
Mucus
The slimy coating on a fish's skin.
