The big idea

Sharks have several rows of teeth that move forward like a conveyor belt. When a front tooth is lost, a new one takes its place, so sharks keep a sharp smile for life.

Why replace teeth?

🦷

Teeth wear out

Shark teeth break and wear down easily, so they are replaced instead of made extra strong.

🏭

A conveyor belt

New teeth are always waiting in the back rows and move forward when needed.

🍤

Different diets

Some sharks use many tiny teeth. The whale shark eats shrimp and plankton.

More about sharks

  • Sharks are fish. Their skeletons are made of bendy cartilage, not hard bone.
  • Shark teeth are a common fossil, so you can find ancient ones on some beaches.
  • There are over 500 kinds of sharks, and many are small and harmless to people.

Words to know

Conveyor belt
A moving strip that carries things forward, like at a supermarket checkout.
Plankton
Tiny living things that drift in the sea.
Fossil
The old remains of a plant or animal turned to stone.
