The big idea

Sharks have no bones. Their skeleton is made of cartilage, which is lighter than bone. A big, oil-filled liver also helps them stay afloat.

How it works

🦴

Cartilage skeleton

Shark skeletons look like other fishes but are made of cartilage instead of bone.

💪

Extra strong

The vertebrae and skull are strengthened by increased calcification.

🛢

Oily liver

A large liver with low-density oil helps stop sharks from sinking.

More about whale sharks

  • All sharks are slightly negatively buoyant, so they sink if they stop swimming.
  • Open-water sharks generally have larger, oilier livers than sharks living near the bottom.
  • Together, light cartilage and an oily liver make swimming more efficient.

Words to know

Cartilage
A strong, bendy material in your nose and ears.
Swim bladder
An air-filled organ many bony fish use to float.

🧪 Try it yourself

Gently bend the tip of your nose or the top of your ear. That is cartilage, and a shark's whole skeleton is made of it!
