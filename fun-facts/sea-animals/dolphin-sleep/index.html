The big idea

A dolphin sleeps with half of its brain at a time. One half rests while the other half stays awake to watch for danger and control breathing, so the dolphin never forgets to come up for air.

Half asleep, half awake

Brain halfStateJob
🧠 One halfAsleep (deep sleep)Rests and recovers. The eye on the opposite side stays closed.
🧠 Other halfAwakeWatches for danger and keeps controlling breathing.

Why sleep with half a brain?

🫧

Avoid drowning

Dolphins breathe air on purpose, not automatically. The awake half makes sure they surface for breaths.

👀

Stay alert

The awake half keeps watch for sharks and other dangers, even during sleep.

🔥

Stay warm

Staying active in cold water helps a dolphin keep its body heat.

More about dolphins

  • Dolphins are mammals, not fish. They breathe air through a blowhole on top of the head.
  • They live in family groups called pods.
  • They find food with echolocation: they make clicks and listen for the echoes.

Words to know

Hemisphere
One half of the brain. A brain has two.
Blowhole
The nose on top of a dolphin’s head that it uses to breathe.
Pod
A family group of dolphins.

🧪 Try it yourself

Try holding your breath for 10 seconds, then breathe out. You can do that on purpose, just like a dolphin! Now think: you breathe even when you are asleep without trying. A dolphin has to be awake enough to breathe every time.
