🦠 Who lives in your gut?

Trillions of tiny microbes live in your gut, and your gut holds up to 80 percent of your immune cells.

The big idea

Trillions of microbes live in your gut, mostly in the large intestine, and up to 80 percent of your immune cells are found there.

How it works

🥦

Fiber helpers

They break down complex carbs and fibers you cannot digest alone.

💊

Vitamin makers

They make enzymes needed for vitamins B1, B9, B12 and K.

🛡️

Defenders

They compete with harmful microbes for space and food.

More about the human body

  • Your mix of gut microbes is unique to you.
  • Babies get their first microbes at birth or from breastfeeding.
  • Some gut bacteria help make chemicals that signal the brain, such as serotonin.

Words to know

Microbe
A living thing too tiny to see.
Immune system
Your body's defense team against germs.

🧪 Try it yourself

Eat a colorful plant food like a carrot or beans today. The fibers in it are a snack for your helpful gut microbes!
